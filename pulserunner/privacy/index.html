Privacy Policy — Pulse Runner

Android game, package com.bumustudios.pulserunner

Effective date: 13 September 2026. Last updated: 4 November 2026.

Controller: Burak Gökgöz (BumuStudios), Türkiye — bumustudios@gmail.com

No account, and your progress stays on your phone. There is no account and no server belonging to this game; the only sign-in is Google Play Games, which is Google's and optional. The game does not read your files, your photos or your contacts — it never asks for a single storage permission. Every level, including the endless one, is computed on your phone, offline.

What does leave your phone is described in full below: Google's advertising service and the ad networks it works with, Google's payment service, Google Play Games, and the usage statistics and crash reports that the developer collects through Google Firebase. If you buy the one-time ad removal, the interruptions stop; the only ad left is the optional second chance you can choose to watch for after a crash.

Who this policy is from

This policy covers the Android game Pulse Runner, distributed through Google Play under the package name com.bumustudios.pulserunner. It is written by the game's independent developer. Questions can be sent to the address in Contact.

The controller of the personal data described in this policy — the party responsible for it under data protection law, including the GDPR and Türkiye's KVKK — is Burak Gökgöz, who publishes the game as BumuStudios, in Türkiye. Contact: bumustudios@gmail.com.

What the game is

Pulse Runner is a one-touch flying game. Hold anywhere and the ship climbs; let go and it falls. That is the entire control scheme. Twenty levels teach and then tighten, and an endless mode keeps going while you last. No account, no subscription, no energy system.

The game runs entirely on your device

This is the most important section, so it is stated plainly:

  • Your runs and your progress are not uploaded. The game has no backend of its own. Your progress and your best results are not sent to any server of the game's own, and there is no leaderboard of the game's own (what Google Play Games receives, if you use it, is described in its own section below). The usage statistics described below record when the game is opened, which screens are shown and for how long — never your runs, your results or your progress.
  • Levels need no server. Every course is generated on your device from the level number alone, so the tenth level is the same tenth level for every player without a single network request.
  • The game reads no files. It requests no storage, camera, microphone, contacts or location permission — not on any Android version.
  • Runs are not shaped by your play. The flight is fully deterministic — a fixed tick with no randomness once a level starts, so the same taps always produce the same run. This is a fairness property rather than a privacy one, but it is part of the same promise: your flying is not analysed — not by the game, and not by the usage statistics described below, which never see it.

What the game stores locally

The game keeps a small amount of state in its own private storage on the device. None of it is personal information, and none of it is sent anywhere:

  • your theme, text size, language, sound, vibration and colour-blind choices;
  • which levels you have unlocked, your best result on each, whether a level is finished, and your furthest endless run;
  • whether the game has already asked for a Google Play rating;
  • whether the one-time "remove ads" purchase is active;
  • a counter of how many interstitial ads have been shown, used to keep ad frequency low.

This local state is deliberately excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so it is not copied to your Google Drive. Google's advertising and consent components also keep their own settings on the device, under Google's control, and the Google Firebase components described below keep three random identifiers in the game's private storage: a Firebase installation ID, the Analytics app-instance ID and a Crashlytics installation ID. Unlike the list above, these identifiers travel with the usage statistics and crash reports. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data removes everything the game has stored, these identifiers included.

Advertising — Google AdMob and its mediation partners

The game is free and is supported by ads served through Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC, depending on your region). AdMob is embedded in the game as a software development kit, and it does collect data, independently of the developer. When ads are served, AdMob and Google's advertising partners may collect and process:

DataUsed for
Advertising ID (a resettable identifier assigned by Android) Ad serving, frequency capping, ad measurement, fraud prevention
Approximate location — coarse, derived from your IP address, not GPS Serving ads relevant to your country or region, and legal compliance
Device information — model, operating system version, language, screen and network characteristics Rendering ads correctly, ad measurement, fraud prevention
App interaction events — that an ad was requested, shown, clicked or closed Ad measurement, billing advertisers, fraud prevention

The game does not request GPS or precise location permission, and it does not send your progress or your play history to AdMob or to any of its mediation partners (below). The developer receives no personal data from AdMob or the partners — only aggregated, anonymous reporting such as impression and revenue totals.

Because ads require an advertising identifier, the game declares the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission. Ad formats used are a banner on the menu and game-over screens, an occasional interstitial between runs, and one optional rewarded ad per run that you can choose to watch (it puts your ship back where it died with the way ahead cleared). An interstitial can appear only right after you tap a button on the card at the end of a run, never by itself during play, and those buttons ignore taps for a moment after the card appears, so a stray tap cannot trigger one. A rewarded ad plays only when you choose a button that says it plays an ad. No ad is ever shown while you are flying.

Mediation partners

The game also contains the software of two other ad networks, which AdMob can call on through its mediation feature. When the game requests an ad, AdMob may pass that request to one of these partners, and the partner that serves the ad then receives the same categories of data listed in the table above (advertising ID, approximate location derived from your IP address, device information and ad interaction events) and processes it under its own privacy policy:

  • Unity Ads — Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy
  • Liftoff Monetize (formerly Vungle) — Liftoff Privacy Policy

In the answers they publish for Google Play's data safety section, Unity Ads says its software may also collect purchase history, user IDs, app interactions (such as taps inside its ads), app usage times (only when Unity's optimisation features are turned on) and diagnostics, for advertising, analytics, fraud prevention and app functionality; Liftoff Monetize says it collects approximate location, for analytics, app functionality and advertising. The game's data safety section on Google Play includes these answers.

Which partner, if any, serves a given ad is decided by AdMob. Your choices in the consent form described under "Your advertising choices" are passed to these partners through the standard consent signal that Google's consent tool stores on the device.

Usage statistics and crash reports — Google Firebase

To see how the game is used and to find and fix crashes, the developer has built two Google Firebase services into it: Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. Google provides them as the developer's data processor, and they are active only in the version of the game distributed through Google Play. The game adds nothing of its own to what they collect — no custom events, no user ID or user properties, no custom keys or log messages — so only their automatic collection applies:

DataUsed for
App-instance ID — a random identifier that Analytics creates for this installation Counting installations and sessions without knowing who you are
Advertising ID — the resettable identifier described above, available to Analytics because the game declares the AD_ID permission for AdMob Usage statistics; use for advertising only as far as your consent choices allow (below)
Approximate location — country or region, derived from your masked IP address, not GPS Statistics by country and region
Device and game information — device model, operating system version, language, game version Statistics by device, Android version, language and game version
Usage events — first open, sessions, screens viewed, engagement time, updates and removal of the game How many people use the game, how often and for how long, and which screens they open
How the game was installed — the Google Play install referrer (for example the store listing or a campaign link) Counting where installations come from
Purchase events reported by Google Play — product, name and price of the one-time "remove ads" purchase Counting purchases; no card or payment details are included
Crash reports, sent only when the game crashes or hits a fatal error — the stack trace, the state of the game at that moment, device details (model, operating system version, orientation, free memory and disk space) and a random Crashlytics installation ID Finding and fixing crashes, and counting how many installations a crash affects

Analytics records that a screen was shown and for how long, not what you do on it, and a crash report shows where in the code the crash happened, not your content. Neither service receives your name, your email address or any account details.

When they run. Both services start on the first launch of the game, in every region, before any consent form has been answered: the game does not hold them back until you answer. Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Analytics runs by default. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, once you answer the consent form described below under "Your advertising choices", Analytics follows your answer, both for storing its identifier on your device ("analytics storage") and for using the data for advertising ("ad user data") and ad personalisation; those two advertising uses stay off there until you agree. You can change your answer at any time from Settings → Privacy options; the entry appears where it is required. Crash reports are sent whatever you answer: Crashlytics relies on the developer's legitimate interest in finding and fixing crashes.

The game has no separate switch for the usage statistics or the crash reports. To object to them, write to the address in Contact; uninstalling the game stops both.

Analytics keeps data linked to an installation's identifiers for no longer than 14 months; aggregated reports that contain no identifiers may be kept longer. Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days.

Google processes this data on the developer's behalf, as its data processor, under the Firebase and Google Analytics data processing terms. It may be processed on Google's servers outside your country, including in the United States, and it is encrypted in transit (TLS).

Achievements and leaderboards — Google Play Games

Pulse Runner works with Google Play Games, a Google service. If Play Games is on your phone and you use it, the game signs you in to it automatically with the Play Games profile Google already holds for you; you can also sign in when you open the achievements or leaderboards from the game, or never. Nothing in the game depends on it.

When you are signed in, the game sends Google Play Games the achievements you unlock and your scores on the game's leaderboards. Google keeps them with your Play Games profile and shows the leaderboards, with your Play Games name and picture, to other players according to your Play Games profile's visibility settings. Nothing else about your play is sent, and the developer receives no personal data from it — only what Google shows publicly on the leaderboards and aggregated statistics.

Your Play Games profile, its visibility and its data are managed in the Google Play Games app and your Google Account; Google's handling of them is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Purchases — Google Play Billing

The game offers one optional, one-time, non-consumable purchase that permanently removes the interrupting ads. There is no subscription and nothing recurring. The rewarded second chance remains available after the purchase — it only ever plays when you ask.

The transaction is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The developer never sees or receives your card number, bank details, billing address or full name. Google Play reports only aggregated sales figures, and Google Analytics for Firebase records that a purchase was made — product, name and price, with no payment details (see above). Inside the game, the purchase is recorded as a single true/false flag on your device, restored from your Google Play account when you reinstall or move to a new device.

Google's handling of payment data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and Google Play's terms.

Updates and ratings — Google Play

The game uses two Google Play libraries. Play In-App Updates asks Google Play whether a newer version of the game is available and, when there is one, lets Google Play offer it to you, download it and install it. Play In-App Review occasionally shows Google Play's own rating card; whether it appears is decided by Google Play, and a rating or review you give goes to Google Play under your Google Account. The game is never told whether you rated it, and the developer sees only the public reviews and the aggregated ratings in the Google Play Console. Both are covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Your advertising choices

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Before personalised ads are requested, the game shows a consent form built with Google's User Messaging Platform, where you choose what you allow, including for the ad partners listed above. Your choice is stored on your device by Google's component and is respected on later launches.

You can change your decision at any time: open the game, go to Settings → Privacy options, and the same form reappears. You may withdraw consent there. Withdrawing consent does not disable the game; you can keep playing, and ads may still be shown in a non-personalised form. Your answer there also applies to Google Analytics for Firebase, as described above; it does not switch off the crash reports.

If you are in a US state with a privacy law

Where a US state privacy law applies (for example in California), the same Google consent tool can show a notice about the use of your information for targeted advertising and let you opt out of its sale or sharing. Where it applies, the Settings → Privacy options entry appears and opens your choices again. Your rights there are described under Privacy rights in US states below.

Everywhere else

The privacy options entry appears only where a consent choice is legally required, so you may not see it. You can still control advertising at the operating-system level: open Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the exact path varies by manufacturer and Android version) to reset your advertising ID, or to delete it entirely, which stops apps from receiving a stable advertising identifier.

Removing ads completely

Buying the one-time ad removal stops the banner and interstitial ads altogether. The only ad left is the rewarded second chance, which plays only when you tap its button; the game loads one in advance so that it is ready.

Permissions the game requests

  • INTERNET — required to fetch ads, to communicate with Google Play Billing and to send the usage statistics and crash reports described above. Nothing else is sent over the network.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — lets the ad components check whether a connection is available before trying to load.
  • com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID — access to the advertising identifier, used by AdMob, its mediation partners and Google Analytics for Firebase as described above.

These are the permissions the game declares itself. The Google and ad-network components built into it add a few more that Android grants at installation without asking you, such as Google Play's billing permission and Android's advertising-services permissions; but the game requests no storage, camera, contacts, microphone, location or account permissions on any Android version.

Legal bases and your rights

Where the GDPR (EU/UK) applies, the legal basis for personalised advertising, including by the mediation partners, is your consent, collected through the form described above and withdrawable at any time. Non-personalised advertising, fraud prevention and the security of the ad services rely on legitimate interests. Processing your purchase is necessary to perform the contract you enter when you buy ad removal. The usage statistics and crash reports rely on the developer's legitimate interest in keeping the game working and understanding, in aggregate, how it is used; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Analytics' storage of its identifier on your device and any advertising use of the data rely on your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)) once you answer the consent form; until you answer, Analytics already runs from the first launch.

Under the GDPR you have the right to ask whether your personal data is processed, to access it, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time, and you may lodge a complaint with the data protection authority of your country. Users in Türkiye have the rights described under Users in Türkiye (KVKK) below.

The usage statistics and crash reports are pseudonymous: they carry random identifiers, never your name or email address, so the developer cannot tell which records are yours. Where the consent form is shown, you can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Privacy options, and you can object to this processing, or ask about it, by writing to the address below. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data ends the link to the old identifiers, and data already sent then expires on the schedule above. Requests concerning advertising data must be directed to Google, or to the mediation partner concerned, as the party that holds it, using the tools in the policies linked above. For anything else, or for help finding the right route, write to the address below.

Security

What the game keeps is stored in its private storage on your device, which Android isolates from other apps. The game has no account and no server, so there is no copy of your progress elsewhere that could be exposed, apart from what you send to Google Play Games. Data sent to Google and to the ad partners travels encrypted (TLS). No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure; keep your device locked and up to date.

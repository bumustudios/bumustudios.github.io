Privacy Policy — PdfPages

Android app, package com.bumustudios.pdfpages

Effective date: 22 August 2026. Last updated: 7 October 2026.

The documents you open and the documents you save never leave your device. There is no account to make, no sign-in and no server belonging to this app. All page editing happens locally, on your phone, offline.

What does leave your device is described in full below: Google's advertising and payment services, and the usage statistics and crash reports that the developer collects through Google Firebase — never your documents, their names or their contents. If you buy the one-time ad removal, the ads stop altogether; the usage statistics and crash reports do not depend on it.

Who this policy is from

This policy covers the Android application PdfPages, distributed through Google Play under the package name com.bumustudios.pdfpages. It is written by the app's independent developer. Questions can be sent to the address in Contact.

What the app does

PdfPages manages the pages inside a PDF. You open a document and its pages appear as tiles you can drag into a new order, delete, rotate, and add to — from another PDF, from a photo, or as a blank page. Several documents opened together become one, which is how merging works. When you are finished you save a new document and can share it. There is no watermark, no subscription and no page limit.

Your documents stay on your device

This is the most important section, so it is stated plainly:

  • Documents are never uploaded. The app has no backend of its own. It does not transmit your PDFs, their contents, their text, their file names or their metadata anywhere, and the usage statistics and crash reports described below never contain them: a crash report shows where in the code a crash happened, not your files.
  • Documents are read through the Android document picker. You pick the specific file you want. The app is granted access only to that file and does not browse your storage. Photos added as pages are chosen through the Android Photo Picker, with the same limitation.
  • A working copy is made in the app's private cache. To draw page thumbnails and to rebuild the document, the app copies what you open into its own sandbox at cache/pdfpages-work/. This copy is what makes editing fast, and it is deleted when you finish with the document, when the cache is cleared, or when the app is uninstalled. Android may also reclaim it at any time.
  • Your original file is never modified. Saving always writes a new document, normally through MediaStore into Documents/PDF Pages on your device's own storage. The file you opened is left exactly as it was.
  • No storage permission is requested on any Android version. The document picker and MediaStore make one unnecessary. On Android 9 and older, and whenever the shared folder refuses a file, the app saves into its own folder on your device instead, which needs no permission; files there are removed if the app is uninstalled.

The app builds its output with PdfBox-Android, an open-source library bundled inside the app. It runs entirely on your device and has no network access of its own; it is a piece of local code, not a service.

What the app stores locally

The app keeps a small amount of state in its own private storage on the device. None of it is personal information, and none of it is sent anywhere:

  • your theme, text size and language choices;
  • whether the one-time "remove ads" purchase is active;
  • a counter of how many interstitial ads have been shown, used to keep ad frequency low.

This local state is deliberately excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so it is not copied to your Google Drive. Google's advertising and consent components also keep their own settings on the device, under Google's control, and the Google Firebase components described below keep three random identifiers in the app's private storage: a Firebase installation ID, the Analytics app-instance ID and a Crashlytics installation ID. Unlike the list above, these identifiers travel with the usage statistics and crash reports. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes everything the app has stored, these identifiers included.

Usage statistics and crash reports — Google Firebase

To see how the app is used and to find and fix crashes, the developer has built two Google Firebase services into it: Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. Google provides them as the developer's data processor, and they are active only in the version of the app distributed through Google Play. The app adds nothing of its own to what they collect — no custom events, no user ID or user properties, no custom keys or log messages — so only their automatic collection applies:

DataUsed for
App-instance ID — a random identifier that Analytics creates for this installation Counting installations and sessions without knowing who you are
Advertising ID — the resettable identifier described above, available to Analytics because the app declares the AD_ID permission for AdMob Usage statistics; use for advertising only as far as your consent choices allow (below)
Approximate location — country or region, derived from your masked IP address, not GPS Statistics by country and region
Device and app information — device model, operating system version, language, app version Statistics by device, Android version, language and app version
Usage events — first open, sessions, screens viewed, engagement time, updates and removal of the app How many people use the app, how often and for how long, and which screens they open
How the app was installed — the Google Play install referrer (for example the store listing or a campaign link) Counting where installations come from
Purchase events reported by Google Play — product, name and price of the one-time "remove ads" purchase Counting purchases; no card or payment details are included
Crash reports, sent only when the app crashes or hits a fatal error — the stack trace, the state of the app at that moment, device details (model, operating system version, orientation, free memory and disk space) and a random Crashlytics installation ID Finding and fixing crashes, and counting how many installations a crash affects

Analytics records that a screen was shown and for how long, not what you do on it, and a crash report shows where in the code the crash happened, not your content. Neither service receives your name, your email address or any account details.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the consent form described below under "Your advertising choices" also covers Analytics: your choices there decide whether Analytics may store its identifiers on your device and whether the data may be used for advertising. Use of the data for advertising ("ad user data") and for ad personalisation stays off there until you consent. You can change your choice at any time from Settings → Privacy options; the entry appears where it is required. The app has no separate switch for the usage statistics or the crash reports; uninstalling it stops both.

Analytics keeps data linked to an installation's identifiers for no longer than 14 months; aggregated reports that contain no identifiers may be kept longer. Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days.

Google processes this data on the developer's behalf, as its data processor, under the Firebase and Google Analytics data processing terms. It may be processed on Google's servers outside your country, including in the United States, and it is encrypted in transit (TLS).

Purchases — Google Play Billing

The app offers one optional, one-time, non-consumable purchase that permanently removes ads. There is no subscription and nothing recurring.

The transaction is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The developer never sees or receives your card number, bank details, billing address or full name. Google Play reports only aggregated sales figures, and Google Analytics for Firebase records that a purchase was made — product, name and price, with no payment details (see above). Inside the app, the purchase is recorded as a single true/false flag on your device, restored from your Google Play account when you reinstall or move to a new device.

Your handling of payment data by Google is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and Google Play's terms.

Your advertising choices

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Before personalised ads are requested, the app shows a consent form built with Google's User Messaging Platform, where you choose what you allow. Your choice is stored on your device by Google's component and is respected on later launches.

You can change your decision at any time: open the app, go to Settings → Privacy options, and the same form reappears. You may withdraw consent there. Withdrawing consent does not disable the app; you will still be able to edit your documents, and ads may still be shown in a non-personalised form. The same choices also apply to Google Analytics for Firebase, as described above.

Everywhere else

The privacy options entry appears only where a consent choice is legally required, so you may not see it. You can still control advertising at the operating-system level: open Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the exact path varies by manufacturer and Android version) to reset your advertising ID, or to delete it entirely, which stops apps from receiving a stable advertising identifier.

Removing ads completely

Buying the one-time ad removal stops ad requests from the app altogether.

Permissions the app requests

  • INTERNET — required to fetch ads, to communicate with Google Play Billing and to send the usage statistics and crash reports described above. Nothing else is sent over the network.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — lets the ad components check whether a connection is available before trying to load.
  • com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID — access to the advertising identifier, used by AdMob and by Google Analytics for Firebase as described above.

These are the permissions the app declares itself. The Google components built into it add a few more that Android grants at installation without asking you, such as Google Play's billing permission and Android's advertising-services permissions; but the app requests no storage, camera, contacts, microphone, precise location or account permissions on any Android version.

Data retention

The developer runs no server and keeps no user data of its own. The usage statistics and crash reports are held by Google on the developer's behalf: Analytics data linked to an installation's identifiers for no longer than 14 months (aggregated reports without identifiers may be kept longer), and crash reports for 90 days. Data held on your device stays there until you clear the app's data or uninstall the app. Data collected by Google through AdMob and Google Play is retained according to Google's own retention schedules, described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Legal bases and your rights

Where the GDPR (EU/UK) applies, the legal basis for personalised advertising is your consent, collected through the form described above and withdrawable at any time. Non-personalised advertising, fraud prevention and the security of the ad services rely on legitimate interests. Processing your purchase is necessary to perform the contract you enter when you buy ad removal. The usage statistics and crash reports rely on the developer's legitimate interest in keeping the app working and understanding, in aggregate, how it is used; storing Analytics identifiers on your device and any advertising use of the data follow your choices in the consent form.

Under the GDPR, and under Turkey's KVKK (Law No. 6698) on equivalent terms, you have the right to ask whether your personal data is processed, to access it, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, and to withdraw consent. You may also lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority — in Turkey, the Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu (KVKK).

The usage statistics and crash reports are pseudonymous: they carry random identifiers, never your name or email address, so the developer cannot tell which records are yours. Where the consent form is shown, you can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Privacy options, and you can object to this processing, or ask about it, by writing to the address below. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data ends the link to the old identifiers, and data already sent then expires on the schedule above. Requests concerning advertising data must be directed to Google as the party that holds it, using the tools in the Google Privacy Policy. For anything else, or for help finding the right route, write to the address below.

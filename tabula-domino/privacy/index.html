Privacy Policy — Tabula Domino

Online domino game for Android, package com.bumustudios.domino

Effective date: 16 October 2026. Last updated: 16 October 2026. Policy version 1.

No email address, no phone number, no real name. You play with a guest account and a display name you choose. Signing in with Google, Apple, Facebook or X is optional and only lets you get your account back on another device.

Tabula Domino is an online game, so your matches are played on our own servers, run by Hetzner in Germany (EU). This page lists everything those servers keep, why, and for how long. There are no ads, no purchases, no push notifications and no Google Firebase in this version of the game. We do not sell your data.

Who this policy is from

This policy covers the Android game Tabula Domino: Online Oyna (shown on your phone as Tabula Domino), distributed through Google Play under the package name com.bumustudios.domino. It is written by the game's developer, BumuStudios, an independent developer, who is the data controller for the personal data described here under Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 ("KVKK") and the EU General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR"). Questions and data requests can be sent to the address in Contact, or through the in-app form under Settings → Account → Support and appeals.

What the game is

Tabula Domino is an online domino game. You are matched with other players by rating, or you open a private table with a code for friends. Matches, ratings and leaderboards are kept on our servers so that they are fair and so that a dropped connection does not lose your game. When no suitable opponent is found, you can play against computer opponents.

This version of the game

This policy describes the version of the game in Google Play's closed test. Some features that the game will get later are not in it, and this policy will be updated, with a new "last updated" date, before any of them arrives:

  • Ads. There are none. When ads are added, this page will name the ad networks and what they collect, and personalised ads will be shown only with your consent.
  • In-app purchases. There are none. Nothing can be bought.
  • Push notifications. The game sends none and registers no notification address.
  • Google Firebase and crash reporting. The game contains no Firebase, Crashlytics or other third-party analytics or crash-reporting component.

Support requests, appeals and moderation

When you use the in-app form (Settings → Account → Support and appeals) we keep the topic, the words you write (up to 2,000 characters), the match or decision it concerns, the app's platform and version, and our reply. Please do not write passwords or other personal details in it. When our team acts on an account — a suspension, a ban, removing a matchmaking wait, hiding a photo — we keep a record of the decision, its reason and which team member made it.

In-app analytics — only with your consent

The game can send usage events, such as "progress screen opened", with the app version, platform and a session number that changes on every launch, only if you allow it. The question appears in the app, and you can change your answer at any time under Settings → Privacy → Help us improve the game. These events go only to our own servers; no third-party analytics service receives them. Your name, IP address, advertising ID, anything you type and the tiles in your hand never enter an analytics event. We keep your answer, the text version it was given for and its time, to respect and prove it.

Separately, our servers count things that already happen on them, such as "match found" and "match finished", to measure how the service runs (number of matches, waiting times). If you decline analytics, or delete your account, these records are kept without your player number.

Technical records

  • IP address. Our application servers use it only in memory: to answer the request and, for at most an hour, as the key of a rate-limit counter that protects the service from abuse. It is never written to our database or linked to your account.
  • Edge logs. The network layer in front of our servers keeps access logs with the IP address and the app's identification string (user-agent) for 7 days, for security and to investigate faults, and then deletes them.
  • Monitoring logs. Our servers' error and monitoring logs may contain a player number, never names or session keys, and are kept for 14 days.
  • App version. Sent with every request so that the server can tell an outdated app to update.
  • Backups. Our database backups are kept for at most 14 days.

What stays on your phone

Your session key (in Android's secure storage), your sound, vibration and theme settings, and the tips you have already seen. The game reads your phone's language to show the interface in it. It asks for the camera or your photos only if you choose to set a profile photo, and requests no location, contacts or microphone permission.

What we do not collect

Email address, phone number, real name, postal address, precise (GPS) location, your phone's contacts, your photos and files (other than a profile photo you choose), voice recordings, payment details, browsing history. We do not process special categories of personal data, such as health data.

What other players see

  • At the table: your display name, profile photo or avatar, country flag, your moves (never your hand), the quick messages you send, and the result.
  • Everyone: your display name, profile photo or avatar, country and rank on leaderboards and in friends lists. A profile photo is visible to other players as soon as you set it; you can remove it at any time in your profile. Other players can report a photo they find inappropriate, and our team can hide or delete it.
  • Your friends: whether you are online or in a match, unless you turn this off.
  • Players you met recently, and anyone who knows your player tag: can send you a friend request.

Bots

When no suitable opponent is found, or in practice, you may play against computer opponents (bots). Bots are always labelled BOT next to their name and on the end-of-match screen; there are no hidden bots. Bot profiles are invented and do not belong to real people.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. Your account is frozen at once and deleted after 14 days; during those 14 days you can open the app and tap Cancel the deletion, and everything comes back as it was. You can also ask for deletion by email without the app. Step-by-step instructions, and the full list of what is deleted and what is kept, are on the account deletion page.

  • Deleted: your display name (also the copies in match records), player tag, avatar choice, profile photo, linked sign-ins, sessions, name history, friends, requests, blocks, invites, quick-message records, matchmaking records, support requests and your analytics answer.
  • Kept without your name, under a random number only: match records and move logs (they are also the other players' records), statistics and rating totals, and records of team decisions, for the periods above.
  • Player number removed as well: analytics events and server-side counts.
  • A report made about you stays until its period ends, with your player number replaced by a new number that cannot be traced back.
  • Deletion reaches our backups when they expire, within 14 days.

Children and protected mode

The game is not directed at children. You must be at least 13 to play. Players aged 13 to 17 play in a protected mode: no personalised ads (once the game has ads), no analytics consent prompt, so no in-app analytics is sent, and no marketing notifications (once the game has notifications). We use the age information you give in the app only to apply these rules. If we learn that a player under 13 has an account, we delete it. If you believe a child under 13 is using the game, write to the address below.

Security

  • All traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted (TLS).
  • There are no passwords. Your session key is kept in your phone's secure storage; our server keeps only its hash, and session keys are never written to logs.
  • Your display name and linked sign-ins are kept in one part of the database; the other parts know only your player number.
  • Your hand and other hidden information stay on the server; your phone receives only what you are allowed to see.

No system is perfectly secure. If a data breach affects you, we will notify the authorities and you as the law requires.

Automated decisions

  • Matchmaking picks your opponent automatically, based on rating.
  • If you cancel many searches in a short time, or do not join matches you were placed in, matchmaking may make you wait a short time before searching again. This is not a sanction; you can still play private tables, and our team can lift it.
  • Reports never trigger an automatic sanction. Every suspension or ban is decided by a person on our team, and you can appeal it in the app.

Your rights

Your right to object. You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your situation, to processing based on our legitimate interest. We then stop, unless there are compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests or the data is needed for legal claims.

Under Article 11 of the KVKK you have the right to learn whether your personal data is processed and to request information about it; to learn the purpose of processing and whether it is used for that purpose; to know the third parties in Turkey or abroad to whom it is transferred; to ask for incomplete or inaccurate data to be corrected; to ask for it to be deleted or destroyed under Article 7; to ask that correction or deletion be notified to those third parties; to object to a result against you that arises solely from automated analysis; and to claim compensation for damage caused by unlawful processing.

Under the GDPR, players in the EU have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability and objection, the right to withdraw consent at any time without affecting earlier processing, and the right to lodge a complaint with the data protection authority of the country where they live.

Privacy Policy: TextLeaf

Android app, package com.bumustudios.textleaf

Effective date: 23 November 2026. Last updated: 23 November 2026.

Controller: Burak Gökgöz (BumuStudios), Türkiye · bumustudios@gmail.com

Your PDF never leaves your device unless you save or share it yourself. There is no account and no sign-in. Opening the PDF, finding and changing its text, placing pictures, drawing and writing the edited file all happen on your phone. The developer runs no server for this app and receives none of your documents. What else leaves your device is described below: Google's advertising service and the ad networks it works with, Google's payment service, and the usage counts and crash reports the developer collects through Google Firebase. None of these receive your PDF, its text, file names or file contents. If you buy the one-time ad removal, ads stop altogether; the usage counts and crash reports do not depend on it.

Who this policy is from

It covers the Android application TextLeaf (package com.bumustudios.textleaf) distributed through Google Play. It is written by the app's developer, BumuStudios, an independent developer. The party responsible for the personal data described here under data protection law (including the GDPR and Türkiye's KVKK) is Burak Gökgöz, who publishes the app as BumuStudios, in Türkiye (bumustudios@gmail.com).

What the app does

TextLeaf edits the content of PDF files: it changes, deletes and adds text, moves, replaces and adds pictures, and adds shapes, lines, highlights, freehand marks, notes, a date and a signature. The edit is written into the PDF itself. It never changes your original file: you save an edited copy. A Tools tab links to the studio's other PDF apps on Google Play.

Your files stay on your device

  • Your PDF is never uploaded. The app does not transmit your PDF, its pages, its text, the edited copy or their names anywhere. The usage counts and crash reports described below never contain them.
  • The PDF is opened through the Android file picker or a share. You choose the file, or share it to TextLeaf from another app; the app gets access to that one file only and copies it into its private working folder. That copy, and the page previews made from it, are deleted when you close the document. Leftovers of a run that ended in a crash are removed at the next start.
  • No storage permission is requested on any Android version.
  • Saving. Save as writes an edited copy through the Android system file picker, only to the place you choose. If you choose a cloud provider, that provider's terms apply to the copy. Share hands a file to the app you choose; what that app does with it is governed by it.

Crash-safe drafts

While you edit, the app writes a draft of your unsaved work, a full copy of the PDF as it is at that moment, into its private storage (a few seconds after your last change, and when you leave the app), so that a crash or a kill does not lose it. The next time you start the app it offers to restore the edits. The draft is deleted after you save, when you discard it, or when you close the document without saving. It is never sent anywhere; no other app can read it. Uninstalling the app removes it.

What else the app stores locally

In the app's private storage, never sent anywhere: the list of recent files (name, the address the system gave the app, page count, date); signatures you chose to keep (up to four small picture files; you can delete each one); your language, default font family and a few display switches; the welcome note having been seen; whether the one-time "remove ads" purchase is active and counters that keep the interstitial ad frequency low; how many saves succeeded, when the app was installed and when it last asked for a Google Play rating, so it asks at most once every 60 days. Backup rules exclude all of it, and every file the ad, mediation and Firebase components keep, from Android cloud backup and device transfer. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes everything; edited copies you saved are your own files and stay where you put them.

Advertising: Google AdMob and its mediation partners

The app is supported by ads served through Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC, depending on your region). AdMob is built into the app and collects data independently of the developer: the advertising ID, approximate location derived from your IP address (not GPS), device information (model, OS version, language, screen and network characteristics) and ad interaction events (requested, shown, clicked, closed), for ad serving, frequency capping, ad measurement, fraud prevention and legal compliance.

Mediation partners. The app also contains the software of two other ad networks that AdMob can call on: Unity Ads (Unity) and Liftoff Monetize (formerly Vungle). The partner that serves an ad receives the same categories of data under its own policy. Unity's published Google Play data-safety answers add purchase history, user IDs, app interactions and, when its optimisation features are on, app usage times; Liftoff's add approximate location. Which partner serves an ad is decided by AdMob. Your consent choices are passed to them through Google's standard consent signal.

The app does not request location permission and does not send your PDFs, their text, file names or file contents to AdMob or to any partner. The developer receives no personal data from them, only aggregated reporting such as impression and revenue totals.

Where ads appear. The app declares the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission.

  • Banner: at the bottom of Home and Settings. Never in the editor, the Tools tab or the welcome note.
  • Interstitial (full screen): at most one after a save has finished, never while you edit, when you open a file or when you leave. A card first says your file is saved and that a short ad comes next; the ad plays only when you tap Continue (or press Back). The first save you ever make shows none, and two interstitials are always at least three minutes apart.
  • There are no rewarded ads and no app-open ads.

How Google handles this data is governed by Google's own policies (Google Privacy Policy; how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services; AdMob and third-party advertising). Unity: Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. Liftoff: Privacy Policy.

Usage statistics and crash reports: Google Firebase

To see how the app is used and to find and fix crashes, the developer has built in Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. Google provides them as the developer's data processor. They are active only in the version distributed through Google Play. Besides what they collect automatically, the app logs four events of its own. They carry only counts and reason codes, never a file name, a path or any text from a document; the app sets no user ID, user properties, custom keys or log messages:

Event What it carries
doc_open the number of pages as a range (1, 2-5, 6-20, 21-100, 100+)
export_ok the number of pages
export_fail the name of the error class (for example an I/O error)
draft_restore nothing

Automatically collected: a random app-instance ID; the advertising ID (only as far as your consent allows); approximate location (country or region from your masked IP address); device model, OS version, language and app version; first open, sessions, screens, engagement time, updates and removal; the Google Play install referrer; the Google Play purchase event of the one-time purchase (product, name, price; no payment details); and, only when the app crashes, a crash report: stack trace, the state of the app at that moment, device details (model, OS version, orientation, free memory and disk space) and a random Crashlytics installation ID. A crash report shows where in the code a crash happened, not your PDF or its text.

When they run: Crashlytics starts at the first launch, in every region, and sends a report only when the app crashes (the developer's legitimate interest in fixing crashes). Analytics is held back until the app knows your consent state; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it follows your answer in the consent form, for storing its identifier and for advertising uses, and you can change the answer any time in Settings, Privacy options. The app has no separate switch for usage counts or crash reports; to object, write to the address under Contact; uninstalling stops both. Analytics keeps data linked to an installation's identifiers for at most 14 months, Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days. Google may process this data outside your country, including in the United States, encrypted in transit (TLS).

Purchases: Google Play Billing

One optional, one-time, non-consumable purchase removes ads permanently. No subscription. Google Play Billing handles the transaction; the developer never sees your card number, bank details or full name. Inside the app it is one true/false flag on your device, restored from your Google Play account when you reinstall.

Updates and ratings: Google Play

The app uses Google Play's In-App Updates library (asks Google Play whether a newer version exists and lets Google Play offer it) and In-App Review (after your second successful save, at least a day after installation and at most once every 60 days it may ask Google Play to show Google's own rating box; the app is not told what you chose). The Tools tab opens the studio's other apps or their Google Play pages; those links carry a campaign tag so that Google Play can report installs that came from them, and no personal data.

Your advertising choices

  • EEA, UK, Switzerland: before personalised ads are requested, the app shows a consent form built with Google's User Messaging Platform, including the ad partners above. You can reopen it any time in Settings, Privacy options and withdraw consent; you can still edit your PDFs, and ads may still be shown in a non-personalised form.
  • US states with a privacy law: the same tool can show a notice and let you opt out of sale or sharing for targeted advertising.
  • Everywhere: Android Settings, Privacy, Ads lets you reset or delete your advertising ID.
  • Removing ads completely: the one-time purchase stops ad requests from the app altogether.

Permissions the app requests

INTERNET (ads, Google Play Billing and update check, usage counts and crash reports; never your PDFs), ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (lets ad components check for a connection), com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID (the advertising ID, used by AdMob, its partners and Analytics). The Google and ad-network components add a few more that Android grants at installation without asking you. The app does not request camera, storage, photos and media, contacts, microphone, location or account permissions, and asks you for no permission while you use it.

Children

A general-purpose utility, not directed at children under 13 (16 in the EEA), not in a children's category. The developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Data retention

The developer keeps no user data of its own: no server, no account. Everything the app stores stays on your device until you delete it, clear the app's data or uninstall. Analytics and crash data are held by Google for the periods above; data Google collects through AdMob and Google Play follows Google's own schedules, and data the mediation partners collect follows theirs.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (GDPR, UK GDPR, Türkiye's KVKK, US state laws) you may ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction or portability of personal data, and object to processing, and complain to your data protection authority. The developer holds no data that identifies you; the identifiers above are random and cannot be tied to a person by the developer. Write to the address under Contact and the developer will say what, if anything, can be done. Requests about ad data go to Google, Unity or Liftoff.

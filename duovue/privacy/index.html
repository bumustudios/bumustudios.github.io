Privacy Policy: Duovue

Android app, package com.bumustudios.dualcam

Effective date: 23 November 2026. Last updated: 23 November 2026.

Controller: Burak Gökgöz (BumuStudios), Türkiye · bumustudios@gmail.com

Your photos and videos never leave your device unless you share them yourself. There is no account and no sign-in. The camera picture, the microphone sound, the layouts and the finished photo or video are handled on your phone and saved to your own gallery. The developer runs no server for this app and receives none of your photos or videos. What else leaves your device is described below: Google's advertising service and the ad networks it works with, Google's payment service, and the usage counts and crash reports the developer collects through Google Firebase. None of these receive your pictures, videos or sound. If you buy the one-time ad removal, ads stop altogether; the usage counts and crash reports do not depend on it.

Who this policy is from

It covers the Android application Duovue (package com.bumustudios.dualcam) distributed through Google Play. It is written by the app's developer, BumuStudios, an independent developer. The party responsible for the personal data described here under data protection law (including the GDPR and Türkiye's KVKK) is Burak Gökgöz, who publishes the app as BumuStudios, in Türkiye (bumustudios@gmail.com).

What the app does

Duovue records photos and videos with the front and back camera of your phone at the same time, and lets you choose how the two pictures are laid out (picture in picture, split screen or one camera). On phones that cannot run both cameras at once, it offers a sequential mode that takes the two shots one after the other. The finished photo or video is saved to your gallery. A Tools tab links to the studio's other apps on Google Play.

Camera and microphone: used only on your device

  • Camera permission. The app asks for it so it can show a live preview and take photos and videos. Camera frames are processed in memory on the phone. They are never uploaded.
  • Microphone permission. Optional, asked when you first record video with sound. If you decline, videos are recorded without sound and everything else works. The sound is written into the video file on your phone. It is never uploaded and never analysed.
  • Saved to your gallery. Photos and videos are saved to your phone's shared gallery (the Pictures and Movies or DCIM area, in a Duovue folder), where other apps you allow can see them. They are your own files: you can share, move or delete them with any gallery app. The app does not upload them or send them anywhere.
  • No storage permission is requested on Android 10 and newer. On Android 9 and older, the system's storage write permission is requested only to save into the gallery.
  • Sharing. Share hands a file to the app you choose; what that app does with it is governed by that app.
  • No location. The app does not request location permission and does not store location in your photos or videos.

What else the app stores locally

In the app's private storage, never sent anywhere: your chosen layout, aspect ratio, resolution and other settings; a note of whether your phone can use both cameras at once (so the app need not test it every time); whether the welcome screen was seen; whether the one-time "remove ads" purchase is active and counters that keep the interstitial ad frequency low; how many captures succeeded, when the app was installed and when it last asked for a Google Play rating, so it asks at most once every 60 days. Backup rules exclude this, and the files the ad, mediation and Firebase components keep, from Android cloud backup and device transfer. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes all of it; photos and videos you saved to the gallery are your own files and stay where they are.

Advertising: Google AdMob and its mediation partners

The app is supported by ads served through Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited / Google LLC, depending on your region). AdMob is built into the app and collects data independently of the developer: the advertising ID, approximate location derived from your IP address (not GPS), device information (model, OS version, language, screen and network characteristics) and ad interaction events (requested, shown, clicked, closed), for ad serving, frequency capping, ad measurement, fraud prevention and legal compliance.

Mediation partners. The app also contains the software of two other ad networks that AdMob can call on: Unity Ads (Unity) and Liftoff Monetize (formerly Vungle). The partner that serves an ad receives the same categories of data under its own policy. Which partner serves an ad is decided by AdMob. Your consent choices are passed to them through Google's standard consent signal.

The app does not send your camera pictures, videos or sound to AdMob or to any partner. The developer receives no personal data from them, only aggregated reporting such as impression and revenue totals.

Where ads appear. The app declares the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission. No ad is shown before your first capture or during a capture. A banner appears on the gallery and settings screens, and at most one full-screen ad may appear after you close the gallery viewer, with a minimum gap between full-screen ads. There are no app-open ads.

How Google handles this data is governed by Google's own policies (Google Privacy Policy; how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services; AdMob and third-party advertising). Unity: Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. Liftoff: Privacy Policy.

Usage statistics and crash reports: Google Firebase

To see how the app is used and to find and fix crashes, the developer has built in Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. Google provides them as the developer's data processor. They are active only in the version distributed through Google Play. Besides what they collect automatically, the app logs a few events of its own. They are: concurrent_probe (whether your phone supports both cameras at once, the reason if not, a feature flag and the Android version; no phone model or chip name), photo_saved, video_saved (length bucket, layout, sound on or off, video width), capture_fail and dual_fallback. They carry only counts and reason codes. They never carry a photo, a video, sound, a file name or a path.

Automatically collected: a random app-instance ID; the advertising ID (only as far as your consent allows); approximate location (country or region from your masked IP address); device model, OS version, language and app version; first open, sessions, screens, engagement time, updates and removal; the Google Play install referrer; the Google Play purchase event of the one-time purchase (product, name, price; no payment details); and, only when the app crashes, a crash report: stack trace, the state of the app at that moment, device details (model, OS version, orientation, free memory and disk space) and a random Crashlytics installation ID. A crash report shows where in the code a crash happened, not your pictures.

When they run: Crashlytics sends a report only when the app crashes (the developer's legitimate interest in fixing crashes). Analytics is held back until the app knows your consent state; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it follows your answer in the consent form, and you can change the answer any time in Settings, Privacy options. To object to usage counts or crash reports, write to the address under Contact; uninstalling stops both. Analytics keeps data linked to an installation's identifiers for at most 14 months, Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days. Google may process this data outside your country, including in the United States, encrypted in transit (TLS).

Purchases: Google Play Billing

One optional, one-time, non-consumable purchase ("remove ads") removes ads permanently. No subscription. Google Play Billing handles the transaction; the developer never sees your card number, bank details or full name. Inside the app it is one true/false flag on your device, restored from your Google Play account when you reinstall.

Updates and ratings: Google Play

The app uses Google Play's In-App Updates library (asks Google Play whether a newer version exists and lets Google Play offer it) and In-App Review (after a few successful captures, and at most once every 60 days, it may ask Google Play to show Google's own rating box; the app is not told what you chose). The Tools tab opens the studio's other apps or their Google Play pages; those links carry a campaign tag so that Google Play can report installs that came from them, and no personal data.

Your advertising choices

  • EEA, UK, Switzerland: before personalised ads are requested, the app shows a consent form built with Google's User Messaging Platform, including the ad partners above. You can reopen it any time in Settings, Privacy options and withdraw consent; you can still take photos and videos, and ads may still be shown in a non-personalised form.
  • US states with a privacy law: the same tool can show a notice and let you opt out of sale or sharing for targeted advertising.
  • Everywhere: Android Settings, Privacy, Ads lets you reset or delete your advertising ID.
  • Removing ads completely: the one-time purchase stops ad requests from the app altogether.

Permissions the app requests

CAMERA (live preview, photos and videos), RECORD_AUDIO (sound in videos, asked when you first record with sound), WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE (Android 9 and older only, to save into the gallery), INTERNET (ads, Google Play Billing and update check, usage counts and crash reports; never your photos or videos), ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (lets ad components check for a connection), com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID (the advertising ID, used by AdMob, its partners and Analytics). The app does not record in the background: if you leave the app while recording, the recording stops and is saved. The Google and ad-network components add a few more that Android grants at installation without asking you. The app does not request location, contacts or account permissions.

Children

A general-purpose utility, not directed at children under 13 (16 in the EEA), not in a children's category. The developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Data retention

The developer keeps no user data of its own: no server, no account. Everything the app stores stays on your device until you delete it, clear the app's data or uninstall. Analytics and crash data are held by Google for the periods above; data Google collects through AdMob and Google Play follows Google's own schedules, and data the mediation partners collect follows theirs.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (GDPR, UK GDPR, Türkiye's KVKK, US state laws) you may ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction or portability of personal data, and object to processing, and complain to your data protection authority. The developer holds no data that identifies you; the identifiers above are random and cannot be tied to a person by the developer. Write to the address under Contact and the developer will say what, if anything, can be done. Requests about ad data go to Google, Unity or Liftoff.

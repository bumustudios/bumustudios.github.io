Privacy Policy — DocLeaf

Android app, package com.bumustudios.docleaf

Effective date: 23 November 2026. Last updated: 23 November 2026.

Controller: Burak Gökgöz (BumuStudios), Türkiye — bumustudios@gmail.com

Your PDF and your Word file never leave your device unless you save or share them yourself. There is no account to make and no sign-in. Reading the PDF, recognising the text of scanned pages and writing the Word file all happen on your phone. The developer runs no server for this app and receives none of your documents.

What else leaves your device is described in full below: Google's advertising service and the ad networks it works with, Google's payment service, the usage counts and crash reports that the developer collects through Google Firebase, and the limited usage metrics that Google's ML Kit text recognition sends to Google. If you buy the one-time ad removal, the ads stop altogether; the usage counts, crash reports and ML Kit metrics do not depend on it.

What the app does

DocLeaf converts PDF files into editable Word (.docx) documents. You pick a PDF (or share one to the app from another app), choose a page range, a layout (keep the page layout, or flowing text) and whether scanned pages are read with text recognition (OCR), and the app writes a Word file in which text, tables and pictures are rebuilt as Word content. A history lists the files you converted. There is no watermark, no page limit set by the app and no subscription. A Tools tab links to the studio's other PDF apps on Google Play.

Your files stay on your device

This is the most important section, so it is stated plainly:

  • Your PDF is never uploaded. The app does not transmit your PDF, its pages, its text, the converted Word file or their names anywhere. The usage counts and crash reports described below never contain them: a crash report shows where in the code a crash happened, not your documents.
  • The PDF is opened through the Android file picker or a share. You choose the file in the system file picker, or share it to DocLeaf from another app; the app gets access to that one file only and copies it into its private scratch folder for the conversion. The copy is deleted when the conversion ends, is cancelled or fails, and leftovers of an interrupted run are removed on the next start, at the latest after six hours.
  • No storage permission is requested on any Android version. The file picker, share intents and MediaStore make one unnecessary.
  • Conversion runs in the background on your phone as a foreground task with a notification that shows the progress and a Cancel button, so that it can finish when you leave the app. Cancelling leaves no output file.

Saving, sharing and opening

A Word file leaves the app's control only where you send it:

  • The result is written through MediaStore into Documents/DocLeaf on your device's own storage. On Android 7 to 9, and whenever the shared folder refuses a file, it is written into the app's own folder instead; the result screen then says so.
  • Save as… opens the Android system file picker, and a copy is written only to the location you choose there. If you choose a cloud storage provider, that provider's own terms and privacy policy apply to the copy you give it.
  • Share and Open hand the file to the app you choose, through a temporary access grant. What that app does with the file is governed by that app.

The Word file contains your document's text, tables and pictures, and standard document properties such as the title (the PDF's file name) and the creation date.

What the app stores locally

Everything below is kept in the app's own private storage on the device, which other apps cannot read, and none of it is sent anywhere:

  • the history of converted files: for each one its name, the location of the saved file, the date, and counts (pages, tables, pictures, pages read by OCR, size). Not its content;
  • the scratch copy of a PDF and the work files of a running conversion, deleted as described above;
  • your language, your default script for scans, and whether the welcome note has been seen;
  • whether the one-time "remove ads" purchase is active, and counters that keep the interstitial ad frequency low;
  • how many conversions succeeded, when the app was installed and when it last asked you for a Google Play rating, so that it asks at most once every 60 days;
  • if you postpone an app update offered through Google Play, which version that was and when, so the offer is not repeated for a few days.

The app's backup rules (backup_rules.xml for Android 7 to 11 and data_extraction_rules.xml for Android 12 and later) exclude all of this, and every file the ad, mediation and Firebase components keep, from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so none of it is copied to your Google Drive or to a new phone. Google's advertising and consent components keep their own settings on the device, under Google's control, and the Google Firebase components described below keep three random identifiers in the app's private storage: a Firebase installation ID, the Analytics app-instance ID and a Crashlytics installation ID. Unlike the list above, these identifiers travel with the usage counts and crash reports.

Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes everything the app has stored, these identifiers included. Word files you saved to Documents/DocLeaf or elsewhere with "Save as…" are your own files and stay where you put them (files in the app's own folder, used below Android 10, are removed with the app). Deleting an entry in the History screen deletes the file it points to.

Text recognition of scanned pages — Google ML Kit

When a page of the PDF is a picture (a scan), the app can read its text with Google ML Kit text recognition, provided through Google Play services, with the Latin, Chinese, Japanese, Korean or Devanagari model according to the script you choose. The setting is called Scanned pages (OCR): Auto, On or Off.

Recognition runs on your device. The page pictures and the recognised text are processed locally and are not sent to Google or to the developer. The recognition models are not built into the app: Google Play services downloads a model the first time it is needed (the app shows "Getting the text recognition model (once)"), over the network, and keeps it on the phone. Under the ML Kit terms the component sends Google limited usage and performance metrics (device and app information, the API configuration, performance metrics, error codes and an identifier) for diagnostics and usage analytics; Google states that it does not transfer them to third parties. This is described in Google's ML Kit data disclosure. Phones without Google Play services cannot read scans.

Purchases — Google Play Billing

The app offers one optional, one-time, non-consumable purchase that permanently removes ads, available in Settings and on the export result screen. There is no subscription and nothing recurring.

The transaction is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The developer never sees or receives your card number, bank details, billing address or full name. Google Play reports only aggregated sales figures, and Google Analytics for Firebase records that a purchase was made — product, name and price, with no payment details (see above). Inside the app, the purchase is recorded as a single true/false flag on your device, restored from your Google Play account when you reinstall or move to a new device. Your handling of payment data by Google is covered by the Google Privacy Policy and Google Play's terms.

Updates and ratings — Google Play

The app uses Google Play's In-App Updates library. It asks Google Play whether a newer version of the app is available and, when there is one, lets Google Play offer it to you, download it and install it. It also uses Google Play's In-App Review library: after your second successful conversion, at least a day after installation and at most once every 60 days, it may ask Google Play to show Google's own rating box. Whether the box appears, and any rating or review you give, is handled by Google Play; the app is not told what you chose. Both are covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

The Tools tab opens the studio's other PDF apps, or their Google Play pages when they are not installed; those links carry a campaign tag so that Google Play can report installs that came from them, and no personal data.

Before personalised ads are requested, the app shows a consent form built with Google's User Messaging Platform, where you choose what you allow, including for the ad partners listed above. Your choice is stored on your device by Google's component and is respected on later launches.

Everywhere else

The privacy options entry appears only where a consent choice is legally required, so you may not see it. You can still control advertising at the operating-system level: open Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the exact path varies by manufacturer and Android version) to reset your advertising ID, or to delete it entirely, which stops apps from receiving a stable advertising identifier.

Security

Your PDFs, their text and the converted files are processed in the app's private storage, which Android isolates from other apps. The app has no account and no server of the developer's, so there is no copy of your documents elsewhere that could be exposed. Data sent to Google and to the ad partners travels encrypted (TLS). No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure; keep your device locked and up to date, and take care where you save or share the Word files.
